Return on Investment Scenarios for Mars-Enabled
          Asteroid-Belt Exploration and Exploitation
                        A long-run economic model for 200-500 year planning horizons
     Prepared as a background-data report; constant 2026 USD; all values are scenario estimates, not predictions.

Executive summary
The physical resource base of the asteroid belt is enormous, but economic ROI is governed by transport
cost, automation, processing throughput, mission cadence, and market-realizable value. The central
bottleneck is not whether asteroids contain valuable material, but whether the material can be extracted,
processed, moved, and sold without destroying its own price.

Mars by itself is modeled as a strategic and scientific cost sink. Its economic value rises when it becomes a
logistics node for asteroid access, propellant production, autonomous industry, and low-gravity
manufacturing.

The earliest plausible positive ROI is not from returning bulk metal to Earth. It comes from water,
propellant, and construction feedstock used in space, because each kilogram used in space substitutes for a
kilogram that otherwise had to be launched from Earth.

The most robust 500-year positive cases are closed-loop space-industrial scenarios where most material
stays in space and only high-value products, strategic feedstocks, data, or specialty goods return to Earth.
Pure platinum-group-metal export is fragile because large supply inflows can collapse the market price.

ROI definition and model boundaries
The model uses the following simple ROI definition:

 ROI = (cumulative gross value - cumulative operating cost - capital investment) / capital investment

The model also includes a 2% discounted NPV ROI interpretation. For very long projects, raw 500-year ROI
can look attractive even when the project is financially unattractive at ordinary discount rates. This
matters because benefits in many scenarios begin only after 80-180 years of investment.

Scenario interpretation
Mars alone is not profitable
Mars-base scenarios are negative in the model unless Mars becomes a logistics and industrial node. A Mars
base can be justified by science, prestige, security, and long-term optionality, but not by immediate
resource return.

The first profitable resource is probably water and propellant
The strongest early case is the NEO water/propellant economy. It does not enrich Earth by importing mass;
it reduces the cost of everything else in space.

PGM mining can fail despite valuable ore
Returning platinum-group metals only works if quantities are managed. Too little output cannot repay
infrastructure; too much output depresses prices.

Bulk metals to Earth are a trap
Iron, nickel, cobalt, and construction metals are better used in space. Returning them to Earth is only
attractive if transport and capture costs fall by orders of magnitude.

Closed-loop space industry is the breakthrough case
The positive 500-year cases mostly come from in-space manufacturing, construction, fuel, servicing, and
strategic systems rather than simple asteroid-to-Earth ore shipments.

Automation decides the upper bound
Scenarios with very high long-run ROI require autonomous mining, self-repairing infrastructure, mass
manufacturing in space, and low-loss transport systems.

Bottom-line model rule
Earth does not get rich because asteroids contain metal. Earth gets rich if space industry becomes
cheap enough that asteroid resources can be processed where they are, with only high-value goods,
strategic materials, and advanced manufactured products returned to Earth.
